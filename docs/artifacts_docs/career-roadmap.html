Working roadmap · Sept 2026

Getting into AI, deliberately

The spine of everything you've built: you build systems that extend what AI can do and that you can operate yourself — orchestration, memory, multi-model tooling. Not scattered breadth. One direction, several instances.

A 6–18 month campaign, not a submit-and-wait. Primary lane you can win now; secondary track kept warm; fundamentals built in parallel through the work itself.

01The primary lane

Applied AI · tooling · agent engineering

This is where your real experience lives and where your hit rate is highest. It rewards "can you ship something that works and reason about it" over CS-fundamentals purity — and your Cloudflare Worker (D1, R2, KV, Queues, Durable Object, Vectorize behind a memory-backed chat system) is real applied engineering, AI-assisted or not. Chosen because it's the strongest match and a genuine on-ramp to the rest, not as a consolation.

Each earns its slot only by evidencing what this lane screens for. Lead with the two strongest.

Interpretability mini-project

03Fundamentals, built in parallel

Learn Python through the projects, not from exercises — you learn faster with a real thing to build. "Enough" for this lane: comfortable reading and writing Python, wiring APIs, and running a small script end to end. That bar is reachable while you apply. The deeper bar (PyTorch, running an ML experiment) is only a hard gate for pure interpretability research — decide later whether that path is worth the investment.

04The bridge — how this opens the rest

Applied AI engineers sit next to research, interpretability, and safety teams, who need people to build eval harnesses, agent scaffolding, and memory systems. People cross over from applied roles because they're already in the building, fluent in the terminology, and known. But the bridge opens through demonstrated adjacent contribution, not proximity alone — the ones who cross built the small safety/interp thing on the side. So: primary lane for the job, one foot always producing artifacts pointed at ML / interp / safety.

05Secondary track — safety / red-team, kept warm

Red-teaming · evalsBio moat

You're genuinely good at stress-testing — you did it to your own analogy this week. This track rewards that plus domain depth over credentials, and your immunology is a real edge. Low effort, opportunistic, climbs a ladder rather than a cold application.

1

Open competitions — start now

HackAPrompt 2.0 (open to anyone, public leaderboard, has a CBRNE track where your biology is a direct advantage), Gray Swan Arena, Kaggle red-team challenges. Little/no code; produces a citable result.

2

Contractor / red-team networks

OpenAI Red Teaming Network, HackerOne/Bugcrowd AI programs, Outlier/OpenTrain domain-expert pools. They pay a premium for medical/bio expertise. Project-based, no credential gate.

3

Eval organizations

METR, Apollo Research, Trail of Bits, Lakera — contractor pipelines with a take-home/eval-exercise screen. "Can you do the work," which favors public results over résumé.

4

Lab roles

Anthropic Frontier Red Team (CBRN/Biosecurity) explicitly wants biologists. By now you apply with a leaderboard record + paid findings + bio moat — a different application than the one that looked impossible.

06Honest caveats

Keep it realistic
Flagship lab roles are very competitive; the ladder exists because cold applications to them are low-probability for anyone. Contractor comp figures in job blogs skew optimistic — treat them as ceilings, not expectations. Pure interpretability research remains the least reachable near-term because of the fundamentals gate; it's reached through this path, not instead of it. None of this makes the plan unrealistic — it makes the sequencing the point.
