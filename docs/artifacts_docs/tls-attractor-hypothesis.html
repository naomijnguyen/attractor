01 — The seed

It starts with one receptor that refuses to mean one thing.

HVEM does not have a fixed function. Engaged by LIGHT it activates; engaged by BTLA or CD160 it inhibits. The same receptor, opposite outcomes — the meaning lives in the partner and the context, never in the node alone.

That is the property worth borrowing. A feature inside a transformer is similarly polysemantic: it has no single meaning, and what it does depends on what else is active and on what is reading from it. So the first correspondence is not decorative. One receptor, context-dependent output, maps cleanly onto one feature, context-dependent effect. This is the foundation everything else is built on — and it is the part that holds hardest.

02 — What the structure actually does

A tertiary lymphoid structure is not in the blueprint. It self-organizes.

LTβR is the organizer. Fired on dendritic cells it drives proliferation and activation of CD4 T cells. Fired on stromal and endothelial cells it drives a chemokine program — CCL19/CCL21 for the T-zone, CXCL12/CXCL13 for the B-zone, plus the vasculature for cells to even enter. Same receptor, three different jobs depending on which cell it speaks through.

Those gradients don't just recruit cells — they sort them. T and B zones segregate, a germinal-center reaction begins, and the structure self-sustains as long as antigen and inflammation persist. A whole functioning lymphoid organ, assembled from local signaling rules, in tissue that was never supposed to have one.

The trigger is transient. The structure outlasts it. That gap is the entire reason the analogy is worth drawing.
03 — The move that makes it work

An attractor doesn't exist in a single pass. There's no basin to fall into yet.

Here is the correction that sharpened everything. It is tempting to map the TLS onto a transformer's single forward pass — prompt in, settled output out. But a single pass has no time axis. It is a transient signal, not a trajectory. There is no surface for the system to settle into, because the surface is carved by the accumulation of turns.

So the single-pass attractor isn't a weak node to be flagged. It is a category error to be deleted. The attractor is definitionally a multi-turn object: the basin is built by sustained contact, the same way a germinal center is built by sustained antigen — neither pre-exists its own history.

cut Single forward pass → attractor basin
Removed, not flagged. No time axis means no trajectory means no basin. The structure only exists in the regime where it can exist — and the absence is the point.
04 — The core claim

Sustained contact builds a structure whose stability comes from responsiveness, not from a fixed input.

A germinal center does not persist because the antigen holds still. It persists because the structure has become self-maintaining and keeps adapting — to affinity maturation, to neoantigens, to whatever the local environment turns into. The stability lives in the structure, not in the stimulus.

Said of a conversation: a multi-turn attractor is not held together by the topic staying fixed. It is held together by the interaction having developed enough structure to keep responding adaptively, regardless of how the content shifts. The container is what's stable. The container adapts. And the local environment is allowed to change underneath it.

Defensible A self-maintaining structure can be stable through continued adaptation — not stable as stuckness. +
Why this holds from the biology

A rut is stable because it stops adapting. A germinal center is stable because it never does. These are opposite kinds of stability that share a word. The healthy multi-turn attractor is the germinal-center kind: it maintains its mode precisely by continuing to respond — the opposite of collapse.

Why it matters for interpretability

It reframes "attractor" from a degeneration story (the model falls into a rut) into a competence story (the interaction sustains an adaptive structure). Those predict different things and are distinguishable in practice — which makes this a claim, not a metaphor.

05 — Where selection comes from

Nothing external grades the response. The competition is the grading.

The obvious worry: if the structure refines over time, what does the refining? In the germinal center, the answer is that nothing outside does. Same antigen yields a spread of affinity variants; the high-affinity clones win the competition for antigen and T-cell help; the low-affinity ones don't get the survival signal and fall away. It self-selects. Duration is refinement — more turns, more rounds of selection, the same way more time in the GC means more affinity maturation.

But the single-axis version of that story is a trap. The same antigen doesn't only produce a better antibody — it produces many kinds of response: class switching, plasma cells, and crucially memory B cells that survive precisely because they are not the current winner. Memory is the structure hedging against the antigen that hasn't arrived yet.

Pure affinity-maximization would destroy the diversity the structure needs to survive its own future.

So the deep claim is not "the best idea wins." It is that a healthy adaptive structure selects for fitness while deliberately preserving diversity it doesn't currently need. In a conversation: holding threads that aren't salient right now but might become the thing the whole exchange turns on at turn forty. That is a richer claim — and a more accurate one — than basin-collapse-to-a-winner.

Defensible Selection is endogenous, and a healthy structure preserves diversity rather than collapsing to the current winner. +
The mechanism, stated plainly

No external judge. Variants compete for a limited resource; fit ones expand, unfit ones lose the survival signal. The multi-turn process itself is the selective environment — which is why this can't happen in a two-turn exchange. There isn't enough sustained contact to select within.

The guardrail against the rut reading

Memory B cells are the proof that the system does not maximize on a single axis. Diversity preservation is what lets the structure answer a question it hasn't been asked yet. A conversational attractor that collapsed to one idea would lose exactly this.

06 — The edge, at three scales
The amber node — one edge, cutting at three scales

There is exactly one open question. It only looks like three.

It is tempting to file the unknowns as a list — what is affinity, what is selection, what makes a representation collapse. But they are not parallel. They are a single thing seen at three depths: a quantity, the operation that runs on it, and the outcome that operation produces. The same edge, resolved at three scales — which is sharper than three separate edges, because it has structure.

In the germinal center, affinity is a physical measurable — binding strength, real on/off rates. When the analogy says an idea "has enough affinity to land in the basin," affinity has quietly become a metaphor for something on the model side that has not been defined or measured. That single undefined quantity is the root. Everything else is what it does.

Open · scale 1 / 3 The quantity. "Affinity" needs a computational definition — here are the candidates. +
Candidate operationalizations
semantic-retrieval distance
Affinity as proximity in embedding space — how close a concept's vector sits to the current context. Concrete and measurable, but possibly the wrong level: retrieval matches stored text, not a concept's grip on the live representational state.
representational overlap / superposition
Affinity as the degree to which a concept is carried by the directions currently active — leaning on superposition and polysemanticity. Closer to the internal-dynamics sense, harder to measure, exactly where interpretability tooling would point.
something third
The honest possibility: neither, and the real quantity is one this framing hasn't named yet.
Intuition

One referent — carbonated sweet drink — is never stored as one token. It lives as a spread of forms (soda, pop, coke-as-generic) and context decides which expresses. That spread is the quantity we're trying to name.

Open · scale 2 / 3 The operation. "Selection" is at least two mechanisms sharing one outcome. +
Two operations, one ending

Frequency selection — which form is ambient here (dialect picks soda over pop). And competitive survival — which variant outlasts the others under pressure (affinity picks a B-cell clone; the rest lose the survival signal). Both terminate in "one thing wins," which is why a single word feels honest. But the process getting there is different, and collapsing them would hide that.

Why it stays amber

Whether these are one abstract operation or two genuinely different things is unresolved — and pretending otherwise would be the exact move this whole map refuses.

Open · scale 3 / 3 The outcome. Context sometimes collapses the superposition — and sometimes sustains it. +
Collapse vs. sustain

Saying "soda" actively suppresses "pop" — expression forces a choice, one reading at the cost of the others. But to find someone annoying and endearing, dense and brilliant is to hold both at once: no winner, no suppression, genuine simultaneity. Like the moon orbiting Earth and Sun — it never has to stop doing one to do the other.

So the deepest question: what determines whether a superposition collapses to a single expressed reading or stays held? When does context disambiguate, and when does it let the mixture ride?

Who decides — all three

Resolved by transformer-internals expertise, not immunology. The biology specifies what each scale must do — confer survival, support competition, permit or suppress coexistence. It cannot say what any of them are inside the model. That handoff is not the argument's gap. It is the argument's most precise point.

07 — What this becomes

An analogy that names its own edge is a research program.

The HVEM correspondence was a seed. Pushed through the TLS — multi-turn framing, endogenous self-selection, diversity preservation, and one edge that resolves at three scales — it stops being a clever mapping and becomes a set of things you could actually go and test:

  1. Does a sustained interaction maintain a stable representational structure while its token-level content demonstrably shifts — a stable container with evolving contents?
  2. Does a healthy multi-turn attractor preserve diversity it isn't currently using — the memory-B-cell move — and does losing that diversity look like the failure mode we'd call a conversational rut?
  3. Which operationalization of "affinity" — retrieval distance, representational overlap, or something unnamed — actually predicts which ideas persist in the basin? (the quantity)
  4. What determines whether context collapses a superposition to one expressed reading or sustains the mixture — soda versus the moon? (the outcome)

None of these require already knowing the answer. They require a structural intuition pushed to a sharp claim, and an honest map of where it needs someone else's expertise to be settled. That map is the deliverable.

On method. The green nodes are claims defended from ten years of TNFSF receptor biology. The amber node is drawn with the same precision as the green ones — not vague hedging, but a bounded question with named candidate answers and an identified path to resolution.

The single-pass attractor node was cut, not flagged, because a cleaner definition dissolved it. That is what the edges are for.

TNFSF → representational dynamics. A working hypothesis, offered as an invitation to test it.
