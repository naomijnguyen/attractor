TNFSF signaling · attention mechanisms

Signal competition as a lens on attention

The HVEM receptor network resolves competing activating, inhibitory, and decoy inputs into a single output. Softmax attention resolves competing tokens into a single distribution. Two places the dynamics genuinely rhyme — and the places they don't, named on purpose.

Scope: an analogy for thinking, not a claim about transformer internals. Immunology is load-bearing; the ML parallels are hypotheses, labeled as such.

Integrating hub

HVEM (TNFRSF14)

A single receptor that integrates competing inputs: it is activated by LIGHT and (on NK cells) CD160, and delivers or receives inhibition through BTLA and (on T cells) CD160. The output isn't set by one ligand — it's set by which ligands win access under the conditions present.

Why it's the center: a limited hub allocating a shared response among competitors is exactly the shape of a softmax — see the first rhyme below.

Where the dynamics rhyme

Two parallels that hold at the level of mechanism, not just vocabulary.

Strong rhyme

Competition for a limited hub ↔ softmax competition

Immunology

LIGHT, BTLA, and CD160 compete for a finite pool of HVEM. More of one bound means less access for the others; the cell's response is the resolved balance.

Transformer

Each query has a fixed attention budget — softmax forces the weights to sum to 1. Keys compete for that budget; raising one lowers the rest.

Shared mechanism: allocation of a scarce, shared resource among competitors, where the winners suppress the losers by consuming what's available.

Boundary: biology isn't normalized to a strict constant sum the way softmax is. The rhyme is "competition under scarcity"; softmax is its exact, sum-to-one form.

Strong rhyme · citable

Decoy sequestration (DcR3) ↔ attention sinks

Immunology

DcR3 binds LIGHT and absorbs it without transducing a signal — a decoy that drains activating ligand out of the system.

Transformer

Because softmax must place its full budget somewhere, models park surplus attention on near-empty tokens (often the first token) that carry it without contributing — an "attention sink."

Shared mechanism: a surplus-signal absorber that exists because the system must allocate an available quantity somewhere — a sink for what has nowhere useful to go.

Honest note: whether sinks arise from softmax's sum-to-one constraint or from upstream "massive activations" is debated — which is itself the interesting open question.

Where it breaks

The parallels above earn their keep by having edges. These are the edges.

  • Active inhibition has no clean counterpart. BTLA and PD-1 deliver a real, driven inhibitory signal. In vanilla attention, a low weight is the absence of signal, not a delivered suppression — softmax can't actively inhibit the way a coinhibitory receptor does. Don't map BTLA onto masking.
  • "Receptor = named component" is a category error. The honest rhymes live in network dynamics (competition, sequestration), not in one-to-one part labels. That's why this version drops the receptor→layer table the earlier draft used.
  • Context-dependent signing (CD160) has no simple analog. The same ligand is activating on NK cells and inhibitory on T cells. There's no vanilla attention primitive whose sign flips with cell state.
Experimental anchor

LTβR is the one node here with wet-lab grounding behind it. LIGHT can be routed to LTβR instead of HVEM, and LTβR agonism drives tertiary lymphoid structure formation — a project I worked on directly. The tempting further leap (TLS ↔ "attractor basins" in latent space) stays explicitly speculative: a forward pass has no literal attractor dynamics. It's flagged as a hypothesis, not folded in as a result.
